Counterexamples to Conjecture 25 and Remark 6 of Garrappa–Gerhold–Popolizio–Simon: Global modulus lower bounds in arXiv:2410.11852v4

Haobo Ma, Wenlin Zhang

Paper

Ma and Zhang examine whether two families of complex functions must always have magnitude at least as large as their value at the corresponding point on the real axis. They give a family of counterexamples to one conjecture and a simple example for another question, whose negative answer they credit to earlier work.

The first family, the two-parameter Mittag–Leffler functions, generalizes the exponential. Its power series adds terms zᵏ/Γ(αk+β), where Γ extends the factorial and parameters α and β determine the coefficients. The reciprocal Gamma factor is taken as zero at nonpositive integers. The series converges throughout the complex plane when α is positive. Fixing the parameters and calling the function E, the proposed comparison is |E(z)| ≥ E(Re z): the left side is a complex magnitude, while the right side is the real value obtained by removing z’s imaginary part. The exponential satisfies equality, making this a natural comparison for its generalizations.

Garrappa, Gerhold, Popolizio and Simon conjectured that, for 1 < α < 2 and β ≥ 0, this global comparison holds exactly when α−1 ≤ β ≤ α, equivalently when the reciprocal function is completely monotone on the positive real axis. Complete monotonicity means that a positive smooth function has derivatives alternating between nonpositive and nonnegative values. The earlier authors already proved that property for the reciprocal throughout the proposed interval. Ma and Zhang challenge the passage from real-axis regularity to complex-magnitude control, not that established theorem.

Their main result covers every α strictly between one and two. At β = α−1, failures occur arbitrarily far along a suitable left-half-plane ray. The mechanism is a missing leading term: near the negative real direction, a contour-integral expansion has zero inverse-first-order coefficient, leaving E(z) approximately C/z² with C positive. The authors use a Hankel contour and bound the error by a constant times |z|⁻³. Compare −r with −r+irt, where t is a suitable fixed positive number. Their real parts agree, but the second point’s greater distance from zero reduces its leading magnitude by the factor 1/(1+t²). For sufficiently large r, the smaller error cannot erase this gap.

Nor is this merely an endpoint defect. At one sufficiently distant point, continuity in β preserves failure throughout a closed interval [α−1, α−1+ε], with 0 < ε < 1. Counterexamples therefore lie strictly inside the conjectured range, where the reciprocal remains completely monotone. Earlier results also give a Bernstein function there by scaling E to equal one at zero and taking its logarithm: it is nonnegative and smooth, with completely monotone derivative. Neither real-axis property guarantees the global bound. The point and interval may depend on α.

A finite certificate gives α = 3/2, β = 1/2 and z = −115+10i. The authors split the first 64 series terms into two rational-coefficient polynomials, one multiplied by 1/√π. Rational enclosures for that constant and exact arithmetic bound the partial sums; a recurrence bounds the remaining even and odd tails geometrically. The complex magnitude is below 0.0000110000004, while the real-axis value exceeds 0.0000299999996. This explicit estimate controls the infinite remainder rather than relying on decimal approximation alone.

The second question concerns Kummer’s function M(a,b,z), another everywhere-convergent power series, with b = a−d and a,d positive; b must not be a nonpositive integer. The manuscript credits the Pudim AI Project’s June 14, 2026 manuscript with an earlier counterexample using small imaginary arguments. Ma and Zhang choose a = 1/2 and d = 2. Cancellation in every coefficient gives M(1/2,−3/2,z) = exp(z)(1−4z/3+4z²/3). At z = 1/2+i√2/2 the quadratic vanishes, whereas at the real part, 1/2, it equals 2/3. Thus the complex magnitude is zero but its proposed lower bound is positive.

The contour, continuity and all-coefficient arguments are written proofs; the explicit certificate uses exact finite arithmetic plus tail bounds. The reported Lean statements cover the parameter family conditional on identifying the function with the source series, a refutation incorporating the series definitions and conjectured equivalences, and negation of the universal Kummer claim. These reported statements do not themselves certify every analytic step; 101 supplementary coefficient checks likewise cannot replace the all-index identity. The endpoint ray refutes an earlier extension of a result allowing exceptions only inside a bounded closed region, while interior failures remain compatible with its original endpoint-excluding scope. Existing right-half-plane bounds are unaffected. A full parameter classification and the listed questions about zeros, convexity, generalized-cosine constants and α ≥ 2 are not settled here.
